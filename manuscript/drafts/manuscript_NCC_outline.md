## 2. Abstract (~150 words; METHANE lead)
Setup: Mangroves are premier blue-carbon sinks; because high salinity is assumed to suppress methanogenesis, their CH4 emissions are treated as negligible and are largely absent from budgets and emission factors. Yet CH4 has rarely been measured across all emitting pathways, and its response to accelerating disturbance is unknown. Approach: component-resolved chamber fluxes (7 pathways) scaled by terrestrial laser scanning and validated against airborne eddy covariance, across an intact->ghost hurricane-disturbance gradient. Key results: intact saline mangrove emits substantially more CH4 than emission-factor assumptions imply, distributed across soils, water and stems; hurricane die-off amplifies CH4 several-fold AND reverses the salinity–CH4 relationship (suppression -> promotion). In CO2-equivalent terms the ecosystem switches from a net radiative sink to a source, with CH4's share of forcing rising from ~1% to ~40% (GWP20). Implication: CH4 emission factors and blue-carbon accounting must include baseline saline-mangrove emissions and disturbance state.

## 3. Introduction (~550 words, no heading; METHANE-forward)
- Mangroves as disproportionate blue-carbon sinks; policy/crediting reliance — BUT they emit CH4, whose potency (28-80x) can erode that benefit.
- **The overlooked-methane gap (pillar 1).** Prevailing paradigm: high salinity/sulfate suppresses methanogenesis, so saline mangrove CH4 is assumed negligible — encoded in IPCC Tier-1 (near-zero >18 PSU) and largely omitted from blue-carbon and wetland CH4 budgets. But emissions are the sum of many pathways (soils, tidal water, stems, roots, deadwood) rarely measured together; component-resolved CH4 in intact saline mangroves is essentially unquantified, especially in the Western Hemisphere. Emerging reports of stem and water CH4 hint the paradigm understates true emission.
- **The disturbance gap (pillar 2).** Intensifying cyclones; Irma 2017 ponding-driven die-off -> ghost forest. Loss of live-root O2 transport, labile dead biomass, and persistent flooding should all favor CH4 — and could break the salinity-suppression relationship — but the magnitude and mechanism are unquantified, and no study closes a disturbed-mangrove CH4 budget against an independent constraint.
- **The leap.** Resolve CH4 by pathway, scale it structurally (TLS), and validate top-down (airborne EC), across an intact->ghost gradient — then place it in net CO2-equivalent terms. Last paragraph: state that intact saline mangrove emits more CH4 than assumed, disturbance amplifies and re-wires it, and the net balance flips.

## 4. Results (~1,500 words; 5 sections, one figure each)

### R1 — Component-resolved methane, even in intact saline mangrove (~300 w) | **Fig. 2**
- 867 chamber fluxes, 7 pathways, wet/dry, gradient. **Intact saline mangrove is not a negligible CH4 source**: emission is distributed across soils, tidal water, and stems at rates that, summed across pathways, exceed what salinity-based emission factors (near-zero >18 PSU) would predict. Establish the baseline first — this is a headline in itself.
- Disturbance then elevates every pathway (soil, water, stem) intact->ghost.
- Transport/structural controls (support the pathway story): stem CH4 declines with height (transport signal), steeper/higher in disturbed forest; dead stems emit >= living; ebullition only in wet-season disturbed water (-> SI). CO2 shown alongside for context (panel b) but not the focus.

### R2 — Structure-explicit bottom-up budgets (~320 w) | **Fig. 3**
- TLS surface areas by component collapse with disturbance (live stem/root area). Scaling rate x area -> stand budgets.
- Ghost budget is ~99% flooded water surface; intact is soil (69%) + roots (23%); **stems are <1% in both** despite the attention. Totals several-fold higher at ghost. Robust to stem height-extrapolation (<2%) and tide assumptions (-> SI).

### R3 — Independent top-down validation (~300 w) | **Fig. 4**
- Airborne eddy covariance (two end-members) as an independent landscape constraint.
- Bottom-up and top-down agree within uncertainty and both rank ghost > intact in the wet season (ghost ~37 vs 51+/-27; intact ~7 vs 29+/-16 nmol m-2 s-1). Same for CO2. Two fully independent methods converge -> confidence a single method can't give.

### R4 — A geochemical regime shift (~280 w) | **Fig. 5**
- Porewater: disturbed sites higher in dissolved CH4, DIC, and salinity; PCA separates classes with CH4 loading aligned to the disturbed axis.
- **Salinity–CH4 relationship reverses**: negative within intact/regenerating (classic suppression), positive within ghost (r=0.6, p=0.03). Disturbance overrides the redox ladder / sulfate suppression.

### R5 — Methane's growing share of net radiative forcing (~320 w) | **Fig. 6**
- Express the CH4 budget as CO2-eq and combine with net CO2 exchange (respiration minus tower GPP) to close the radiative balance.
- Lead with methane's SHARE: even in intact forest CH4 is a non-negligible ~1% (GWP100) but a meaningful ~3-4% at policy-relevant GWP20, rising to ~19% (GWP100)/~40% (GWP20) in ghost forest — i.e., CH4 becomes a first-order warming agent after disturbance.
- Net balance: intact = radiative sink (-4.6 kg CO2-eq m-2 yr-1; validated vs tower NEP), ghost = source (+1.8) — the title's switch. Note plainly the CO2 sink loss is expected; the point is that CH4 turns "less uptake" into active, methane-amplified warming.
- Scale by the Irma dieback footprint -> regional CH4 (and net) forcing perturbation (landscape panel).

## 5. Discussion (~750 words; METHANE-forward)
- **Saline mangrove methane is underestimated.** Lead here: component-resolved measurement shows intact saline mangrove emits more CH4 than the salinity-suppression paradigm and Tier-1 emission factors assume; stems and tidal water are systematically missed pathways. Consequence: current blue-carbon/wetland CH4 budgets under-count a real term even before disturbance.
- **Disturbance re-wires methane and breaks the salinity rule.** Die-off amplifies CH4 several-fold and inverts salinity–CH4 (suppression -> promotion); mechanism (flooding, labile dead C, lost root O2 transport). Generalizes to other degrading/saltwater-intruded wetlands and ghost forests worldwide.
- **Emission factors & accounting.** CH4 emission factors need (i) a non-zero baseline for intact saline mangrove and (ii) a disturbance-state dependence; blue-carbon crediting/NBS need permanence risk under rising cyclone intensity + SLR. (This is the NCC hook.)
- **Net balance in context.** The sink->source flip follows once CH4 is added to the (expected) loss of CO2 uptake — but methane is what makes a degraded mangrove an *active* warming source, not merely a lapsed sink; its share grows on policy-relevant horizons.
- **Method advance.** First multi-scale, closure-validated CH4 budget across a disturbance gradient; transferable template.
- **Caveats.** NEE/forcing not burial; regenerating class + full seasonality unresolved; ghost GPP~0 (upper-bound source); lateral DIC export.
- **Outlook.** Burial + lateral fluxes; revised CH4 emission factors; microbial metagenomes of hypersaline methanogenesis.
